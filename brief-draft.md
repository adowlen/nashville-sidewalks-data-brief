# Nashville's sidewalk inventory: 1 in 4 inventoried segments is recorded Fair or Poor, and the inventory's two repair fields cover different segments

**One-line summary:** Of the 14,938 sidewalk segments in Nashville's Sidewalks Inventory, 9,399 (62.9 percent) are recorded Good, 2,308 (15.5 percent) Fair, and 1,454 (9.7 percent) Poor, with 1,777 (11.9 percent) carrying no usable condition value; a repair score is recorded for 97.3 percent of Poor segments, while a repair construction cost is recorded for only 69 of the 1,454 Poor segments (4.7 percent) and for 1,520 of the 9,399 Good segments.

Start with the scale. The inventory holds 14,938 segments totaling 7,400,787.5 feet of recorded length - 1,401.7 miles (derived) - across all 35 Metro Council districts. Machine Readable pulled the full layer from Metro's ArcGIS service on November 10, 2026; the pulled row count matches the server's own count for the layer exactly (14,938). One row is one sidewalk segment as recorded in the inventory, identified by its on-street and its from-street and to-street. The inventory records segments that exist in the inventory; it contains no record for a street with no sidewalk, so nothing in this brief measures where sidewalks are missing.

## Condition, counted two ways

- As recorded and normalized (see Methods for the normalization rule), Condition reads: Good 9,399 (62.9 percent of all 14,938 segments), Fair 2,308 (15.5 percent), Poor 1,454 (9.7 percent), and Unknown 1,777 (11.9 percent). Fair and Poor together are 3,762 segments (25.2 percent of all segments; 28.6 percent of the 13,161 segments with a determined condition). Human scale (derived): about 1 in 4 inventoried segments is recorded Fair or Poor.
- Counted by length instead of by segment, on the 14,900 segments with a recorded length: Good holds 62.0 percent of the total length (869.3 miles, derived), Fair 18.0 percent (252.9 miles, derived), Poor 10.1 percent (141.6 miles, derived), and Unknown 9.8 percent (137.8 miles, derived). The length shares and the segment shares are printed separately throughout this brief; they have different bases and are not interchangeable. Fair and Poor together account for 394.5 miles of recorded length (derived).
- The raw Condition field holds 12 distinct values. Alongside GOOD (9,010), FAIR (2,308), and POOR (1,454), the field carries Good (386), a single-space value (992), a blank value (770), NA (13), and five one-record variants (a leading-space GOOD, a trailing-space Good, gOOD, Goo, and the value 31). The normalization merges case and spacing variants of Good, Fair, and Poor; blank, space, NA, 31, and the truncated value Goo are counted as Unknown. The full raw counter is in data/condition_raw_panel.csv.

## Where Poor segments concentrate

District figures use the 14,757 segments whose District field names exactly one council district; 150 segments name two or three districts and 31 name none or an unreadable value, and those 181 segments are excluded from every district figure below (see Methods).

- By share, District 11 records the highest Poor share: 175 of its 570 segments (30.7 percent) are Poor, and 279 (48.9 percent) are Fair or Poor. District 18 records 116 Poor of 498 (23.3 percent) and the highest Fair-or-Poor share, 273 of 498 (54.8 percent). District 17 records 172 Poor of 811 (21.2 percent).
- By count, District 21 holds the most Poor segments, 212 of its 1,133 segments (18.7 percent), followed by District 19 with 186 of 1,660 (11.2 percent). District 19 is the largest district in the inventory by segment count.
- By street, Nolensville Pike carries 37 Poor segments and Gallatin Pike 22; the next streets are 24th Ave N (15), 4th Ave S (14), and Cleves St (14). These are counts of segments as recorded, not lengths or rates.
- Coverage varies by district in a way that limits comparison. District 31 records 0 Poor segments among its 923, with 111 (12.0 percent) Unknown condition and a repair score on only 13 segments. District 33 records 11 Poor among its 1,001 (1.1 percent), with 265 (26.5 percent) Unknown condition and a repair score on only 33 segments. In most other districts a repair score is recorded for a substantially larger share of segments, so the low Poor counts in Districts 31 and 33 are printed alongside their coverage, not as a ranking result.

## The repair score field

- RepairScoreWalkNBike is recorded as a positive value on 3,720 segments (24.9 percent of all 14,938); it is 0 on 8,859 segments and missing on 2,359, and 0 and missing are treated as not scored throughout this brief. Every recorded positive value is a multiple of 5, from 5 to 175. On the 3,720 scored segments, the median score is 90, the mean is 90.6, the 75th percentile is 115, and the 90th percentile is 130.
- The score field covers Fair and Poor segments almost completely and Good segments almost not at all: 1,415 of 1,454 Poor segments (97.3 percent) and 2,276 of 2,308 Fair segments (98.6 percent) carry a positive score, against 29 of 9,399 Good segments (0.3 percent). The median score among scored Poor segments is 110; among scored Fair segments it is 80.
- By district, on scored segments only: District 19 holds the highest total score, 54,860 across 526 scored segments (mean 104.3). District 21 holds the highest mean among districts with 30 or more scored segments, 113.1 across 445 scored segments (total 50,315), followed by District 17 at 112.6 across 351 scored segments (total 39,540). District totals and means have different bases (the count of scored segments in each district) and are printed with those bases here and in data/district_panel.csv.

## The repair cost field does not sit on the same segments

- RepairConstructionCost is recorded as a positive value on 1,771 segments (11.9 percent of all 14,938); it is 0 on 10,808 segments and missing on 2,359, and 0 and missing are treated as no recorded cost throughout this brief. On the 1,771 cost-carrying segments, the recorded costs sum to $59,743,161.85, with a median of $25,267.91, a mean of $33,734.14, and a maximum of $850,143.79.
- That sum is not distributed the way the condition counts are. Of the $59,743,161.85, $54,700,748.26 (91.6 percent, derived) sits on segments recorded Good: 1,520 Good segments carry a cost, with a median of $27,038.26. Fair segments carry $3,920,455.86 across 182 segments, and Poor segments carry $1,121,957.72 across 69 segments. Only 263 segments carry both a positive repair score and a positive repair cost.
- The recorded values also repeat in a pattern consistent with formula-generated estimates rather than per-segment quotes: the 1,771 positive costs hold only 765 distinct values, the single most common value appears on 34 segments, and 202 cost-carrying segments share a value that appears on 10 or more segments. District 19 carries the largest district cost sum, $18,185,703.02 across 421 cost-carrying segments, followed by District 21 at $10,962,562.32 across 337.
- Because the cost field is populated on 11.9 percent of segments and sits mostly on Good segments, the $59,743,161.85 sum is reported in this brief only as the sum of the recorded field on its cost-carrying base. It is not presented as the cost of repairing the Fair and Poor network, and no backlog total is computed from it.

## Repairs on record, and width

- DateRepaired is recorded on 2,182 segments (14.6 percent). The recorded dates run from December 31, 1998 to June 6, 2024 (America/Chicago local dates); no segment in this snapshot carries a repair date after June 6, 2024. Among the 2,182 repair-dated segments, 1,792 (82.1 percent) are currently recorded Good, against 59.6 percent Good among the 12,756 segments with no repair date - the internal control described in verification.md.
- SidewalkWidth is a text field, not a number. Of the 14,938 segments, 11,881 carry a single numeric value (median 5 feet; the most common value is 5 feet, on 7,768 segments), 1,771 carry a range written as text (for example 5-6), and 1,286 are blank. On the single-value base, 936 segments (7.9 percent) record a width under 5 feet. Range values are counted as ranges and are not averaged in this brief.

## How this network sits against the crash record

These comparisons use only figures published in the earlier Machine Readable traffic accidents brief, at city level, with that brief's bases printed; the two layers use different geographies, so no district-level comparison is made.

- Nashville Traffic Accidents (published November 2, 2026): 222,903 reported crashes from January 1, 2019 to October 31, 2026, with 926 people killed, of whom 280 (30.2 percent) were pedestrians. Repository: https://github.com/adowlen/nashville-traffic-accidents-data-brief
- The sidewalk inventory's District field is a Metro Council district. The crash layer's published geography is precinct and ZIP code, not council district, and this package holds no verified crosswalk between the two. The brief therefore does not join crashes to sidewalk condition by district, and no figure in this brief should be read as a crash rate for a sidewalk condition class: the crash layer and the inventory also have different populations (reported crashes, 2019-2026, against a current segment inventory), and the inventory contains no walking-volume or exposure measure.

## Methods

Population: all 14,938 records in the Sidewalks_View layer (ArcGIS FeatureServer layer 0, services2.arcgis.com/HdTo6HJqh92wn4D8), pulled November 10, 2026 with 1,000-record pagination ordered by OBJECTID; the pulled count was verified against the server's returnCountOnly count (14,938) at pull time. The service listing shows the layer's data was last edited November 9, 2026. Length: the Shape__Length field's alias in the layer metadata is "Length (ft)" and the service spatial reference is EPSG:2274 (Tennessee State Plane, feet), so lengths are reported in feet as served; every mile figure is derived (feet divided by 5,280) and labeled derived. Condition normalization: the recorded value is trimmed and case-folded; Good, Fair, and Poor variants merge; blank, single-space, NA, 31, and Goo are counted as Unknown (1,777 segments) and carried on the full 14,938-segment denominator in every condition share. District normalization: a segment enters a district figure only when its District field names exactly one district number from 1 to 35 (14,757 segments); 150 segments name two or three districts, and 31 name none or an unreadable value (one District value reads Good). Score and cost: 0, negative, and missing values in RepairScoreWalkNBike and RepairConstructionCost are treated as not recorded and excluded from score and cost bases, which are printed wherever a score or cost figure appears. SidewalkWidth is a text field and is reported as recorded: single numeric values, range strings, and blanks are counted separately. Dates are epoch-millisecond values in the service, converted to America/Chicago local dates. No join to any other dataset was performed. Category stability: the layer is a current inventory snapshot with no condition time series, so no trend is computed; the score and cost fields are populated on a subpopulation (each field is missing on 2,359 segments and the two are jointly missing on 2,358 of them; 1,763 of the jointly missing segments carry blank, space, 31, or truncated condition values) and the brief treats every score and cost figure as describing that subpopulation. Exposure: the inventory contains only inventoried sidewalk segments; it contains no count of streets without sidewalks and no walking-volume measure, so no completeness rate and no crash rate is computed. Full audit, counters, and verification: verification.md.

## What this might mean

One reading is that the inventory was built to prioritize repair work: the score field covers Fair and Poor segments almost completely, which is the pattern a prioritization pass over the segments needing work would produce, and the cost field may record a different quantity - for example construction cost associated with segments in a capital or development context, most of which are recorded Good - rather than the repair cost of the scored segments. The strongest alternative explanation is that the two fields were populated in separate passes over separate subpopulations at separate times, and neither field's coverage reflects a single current program: the repair dates end in June 2024, the score and cost fields are missing on the same 2,359 segments, and district coverage varies sharply (Districts 31 and 33 are largely unscored). The inventory alone cannot separate the two readings, and this brief reports each field on its own printed base for that reason.

## Who can act

- **Nashville Department of Transportation and Multimodal Infrastructure (NDOT)** controls the sidewalk inventory and the repair program it feeds. Concrete action: publish the definitions and population rules for RepairScoreWalkNBike and RepairConstructionCost - which segments each field is meant to cover - and complete condition and score coverage in the districts where it is partial (District 33 records 265 Unknown-condition segments of 1,001; District 31 records a score on 13 of 923). Cost class: low-cost operational change (documentation and inventory completion within the existing program). Outcome measure: field definitions in the layer metadata at the service URL in sources.md, and the Unknown-condition and unscored counts recomputed on a later snapshot of this layer.
- **Metro Council** controls sidewalk funding decisions, including under the Choose How You Move transportation program. Concrete action: use the district figures with their printed bases in district-level funding discussions - District 11 records a 30.7 percent Poor share (175 of 570 segments), District 21 the largest Poor count (212) and the highest mean repair score (113.1 on 445 scored segments) - and request a cost figure computed on the Fair and Poor population before treating any recorded cost sum as a backlog figure. Cost class: budget decision. Outcome measure: the Fair-or-Poor count and share by district (data/district_panel.csv) recomputed on the next inventory snapshot. The Council next meets Tuesday, November 17, 2026, and a Charter Revision special meeting is scheduled November 13, 2026.
- **Choose How You Move program staff** control the sidewalk construction and repair sequencing under the program. Concrete action: sequence published repair priorities from the score field, which covers 97.3 percent of Poor segments, and publish the crosswalk used if program reporting joins crash data to council districts, so a district-level safety comparison can be computed on a stated basis. Cost class: major policy change (program sequencing at program scale; no cost figure is invented here - the only cost figures in this brief are the recorded field values on their printed bases). Outcome measure: a published district crosswalk and the scored-Poor coverage rate recomputed on a later snapshot of this layer.
